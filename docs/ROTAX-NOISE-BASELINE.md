User-reported peak readings from an iPhone and a separate sound meter. Units are reported dB. Weighting, response setting, calibration and exterior microphone positions were not supplied. The instruments were used for rough comparison. The user identified these as peaks around 244 Hz. A full frequency spectrum and exact band levels were not recorded.

## Operating condition

The test used Rotax exhaust on the dyno. The loudest perceived event occurred shortly after the dyno brake and throttle were released, as RPM began falling from its peak. The user reports that sound falls significantly afterward. Inside the dyno room, the user reports sound above the instruments' usable range (roughly 110–120 dB). Treat this as an over-range observation. The specific Rotax engine and exhaust configuration were not supplied.

## What to check on a repeat run

Exclude the original “Exhaust In” test from comparisons. Air was flowing over the microphones at the inside measurement position even with the fan off. Gently blowing across the microphones reproduced the elevated response in a later test.

- Inline had the highest meter reading among the ordinary outlet checks, at 94 dB. Use a fixed receiver position to compare which outlet contributes most to outdoor noise.
- Keep the taped-inlet readings as standalone observations. To assess the 6-inch branch, repeat the open and taped inlet conditions at the same probe position and operating condition with the microphone outside the airflow.
- Keep microphone position, weighting, response setting and RPM/load sequence fixed. Capture the release transient and frequency bands with an instrument that covers the expected level. Published Rotax spectra would need to match the engine, exhaust and operating condition to be comparable.
